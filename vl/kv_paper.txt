To overcome these limitations, we introduce a learnable strategy for visual token pruning that replaces heuristic scoring rules, aiming at more
  accurate and robust token importance estimation. Our design is motivated by an empirical observation: attention scores computed in the deep layers
  of the LLM provide a substantially more reliable signal of visual token importance; however, directly computing these scores requires an additional
  full prefilling pass, which incurs extra computational and memory costs and undermines the very efficiency that token pruning aims for. To resolve
  this dilemma, we propose \textbf{DAS} (\textbf{D}eep \textbf{A}ttention \textbf{S}urrogate), a lightweight, plug-and-play framework that predicts
  deep-layer attention scores to guide visual token pruning. Specifically, we first identify a small subset of visual attention heads whose
  last-layer attention faithfully localizes query-relevant visual evidence, and use their scores as supervision signals. DAS is then trained to
  predict these scores directly from the input visual and textual features, enabling query-aware token importance estimation at negligible cost
  without explicitly computing deep-layer attention. In addition, DAS introduces a boundary-aware distillation loss that concentrates supervision on
  tokens near the retention threshold, where estimation errors most affect the pruned context. Extensive experiments on XX benchmarks demonstrate
  that DAS consistently outperforms state-of-the-art pruning methods under identical retention budgets, improving over
  VisionSelector~\cite{visionselector} by XX\% on ChartQA and XX\% on TextVQA at a 20\% retention ratio.
